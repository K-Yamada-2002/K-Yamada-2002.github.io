Hello. I write under the name Ewald. I usually study mathematics as an undergraduate mathematics major, and after listening to comments from mathematicians and mathematics students around me, I began to feel that **the impact AI will have on mathematical research is being severely underestimated**. That is why I decided to write this essay.

Accordingly, I do not think this essay represents the average opinion of people who do mathematics. Still, I hope it may serve as a reference for what a singularitarian student of mathematics is thinking.

## Overview

Within the next few decades, and probably **within the 2020s, mathematics done by biological humans will come to an end.** At first glance this may look like something to mourn, but in fact it is good news for everyone who wishes to explore the world of mathematics. By using AI, and by **merging with AI**, humanity will be able to realize greater rigor than ever before, expand mathematical intuition, and explore domains that have not yet been touched. **Mathematical exploration is literally infinite**, and together with AI we will be able to enjoy that vast world more than ever.

## Current Underestimation and Possibility

In the paper by OpenAI researchers analyzing the labor-market impact of LLMs [1][2], mathematicians were listed among the occupations most exposed to these systems. This is still fresh in memory. However, in the Japanese mathematics community and on social media as I observe them, many people seem skeptical of that result, and one can even find a tendency to ridicule it.

The main reason they reject this kind of analysis can be summarized as follows: "mathematics requires rigorous reasoning ability, and LLMs are bad at that." Indeed, when current LLMs such as GPT-4o are asked to solve mathematical problems, their logic often becomes incoherent.

But it is shortsighted to infer the limits of AI from the present performance of systems that are not even specialized for mathematics. Imagine, for example, being asked to answer a difficult mathematical problem immediately, nonstop, without being allowed to use pencil and paper. We should be careful about anthropomorphizing LLM behavior too easily, but what present LLMs are doing seems close to that kind of task.

Is there any prospect of improving such weaknesses? At present, the importance of so-called System 2 thinking, that is, conscious and logical thought, is widely recognized in the effort to realize AGI, and research is moving in that direction [3]. In mathematics, however, collaboration with proof assistants such as Coq and Lean is clearly promising.

Even existing research already provides examples. GPT-f [4] found short new proofs accepted into the Metamath library, and AlphaGeometry [5], which combines a symbolic reasoning engine with a language model, achieved performance comparable to a gold medalist on mathematical olympiad geometry problems.

Since the advent of LLMs, research applying deep learning to theorem proving has grown rapidly [6]. I think it is entirely possible to solve, or at least render harmless, **LLM hallucination in mathematical tasks**. If that happens, it is obvious that the work of mathematicians could be significantly affected.

## When Is X-Day?

When Ray Kurzweil predicted in his 1999 book [7] that machines with human-level intelligence would appear by 2029, almost no AI researchers agreed with him [8]. But 25 years later, many AI researchers and business leaders now predict, almost in step with one another, that AGI will arrive in the second half of the 2020s, or at least say that they would not be surprised if it did.

Among people connected to OpenAI who have often made relatively explicit future forecasts, examples include James Betker (2027-2029) [3], Leopold Aschenbrenner (2027) [9], John Schulman (2026-2027) [10], Daniel Kokotajlo (2026) [11], Zack Kass (2027) [12], and Sam Altman (from 2028 onward) [13].

Outside OpenAI, one can also name major figures such as Demis Hassabis (by around 2034) [14], Jensen Huang (by around 2028) [15], Dario Amodei (2025-2026) [16], and Elon Musk (by around 2026) [17]. The definition of AGI varies somewhat from person to person, but it is clear that **many top AI researchers and executives predict the realization of AGI in the late 2020s**.

Of course, the future cannot be predicted with certainty. But when this many people, including experts, make such predictions, we should take seriously the possibility that **AGI capable of performing every intellectual task at or above the human level may appear within a few years**.

What happens if we restrict attention to mathematics? Modern mathematics is an extremely advanced intellectual task, so one might think that AI surpassing top mathematicians would appear at the same time as AGI, or perhaps slightly later. I think that is unlikely. Rather, **AI surpassing mathematicians may be feasible several years before the birth of AGI**.

For example, there is a report that by combining Monte Carlo tree search with an LLM, Llama 8B achieved mathematical reasoning ability comparable to GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and other frontier models [18]. Llama 8B has roughly one hundredth as many parameters as those frontier models, suggesting that when specialized for mathematical reasoning, **one may be able to obtain comparable mathematical ability with about 1% to 10% of the compute needed for AGI**.

Leopold Aschenbrenner argues that AGI by 2027 is highly realistic, and also states that by the end of 2027, computing power roughly 100 to 1000 times the present level may be available [9]. In other words, from the perspective of compute alone, **AGI is estimated to require roughly 100 to 1000 times current compute**. Putting these considerations together, **the compute needed for AI surpassing mathematicians may already exist as of 2024**.

Of course, Aschenbrenner's forecast includes expected improvements in model architecture and other factors, so I think it is unlikely that AI surpassing mathematicians will appear during 2024 or in the first half of 2025. But **that possibility cannot be ruled out**. Personally, I think **around 2026** is a reasonable overall estimate.

## From Opposition to Merger

If there is a possibility that AI surpassing the top human mathematicians will appear in the very near future, within two or three years, how should mathematicians and students majoring in pure mathematics act?

First, continuing to search for things human mathematicians can do but AI cannot may be important for AI research, but **it is not very meaningful for mathematicians**.

For example, when shogi engines first began to surpass humans, there were problems such as their inability to respond to certain trap-like human moves. Today, however, it is almost impossible for humans to win by using such methods. The same applies to mathematical AI. It is better to assume that **roughly anything humans can do, AI can also do**, and that its weaknesses will soon be resolved.

Then might there still be a possibility like this? "Mathematical AI certainly surpasses human mathematicians in every respect. It can output formally verified proofs of difficult problems such as the Millennium Prize Problems, generate new conjectures, and create new theories. But because these results are difficult for humans to understand, human mathematicians will still have a role in interpreting them."

At first glance this sounds plausible, but I think the possibility is unfortunately low. That is because **soon after AI surpassing mathematicians appears, AGI capable of performing all intellectual tasks at or above the level of top humans is also expected to appear**. Such AGI will also be better than humans at interpreting and explaining the results of mathematical AI.

Nor is it easy to find meaning in mathematics being performed by biological humans in the way one might for sports or shogi. It is true that many mathematicians are distinctive personalities and can become popular public figures, but there are almost no cases where they become popular because their mathematical ability itself is being evaluated by a broad audience.

Then what should mathematicians and mathematics students do? I think that **people who love mathematics should merge with machines**. Of course, connecting machines to the human brain and extending human intelligence is an extremely difficult project. It will probably become possible only after AI surpassing mathematicians or AGI appears, so we will need to wait for some time, but we can support research related to it.

Such technology is likely to be completed by superintelligence born through repeated self-improvement after AGI. Therefore, questions of superintelligence alignment and governance become important, but research in these areas is still developing and there are too few researchers. For that reason, even individuals may be able to have a positive impact by donating to such organizations or by contributing using their mathematical knowledge.

The subtitle of Kurzweil's 2024 book [19] was, by coincidence, "When We Merge with AI." Discussions of AI and the Singularity tend to focus only on "machines exceeding human abilities" and to be trapped in a machine-versus-human framing, but **we should look beyond that opposition toward the horizon where humans transcend biological limits**.

## The Infinity of Mathematics

Board games without chance elements, such as shogi and Go, theoretically have "complete solutions." Even as of 2024, there are reports that checkers [20] and Othello [21] are draws if both players continue to play optimally. Could mathematics as an activity similarly be "solved" by AI?

Fortunately for those who love mathematics, **that cannot happen**. First, there are infinitely many possible mathematical problems. Natural numbers themselves are already infinite.

And they are not merely infinite in number. Questions of the form "Is the digit in the nth decimal place of 10 / 99 equal to 1?" exist for every natural number, and hence infinitely many times, but they have the simple answer "YES if n is odd, NO if n is even." Infinite questions alone could still be boring in this way, but **mathematics is not like that, as has already been proved**.

For example, as an application of Godel's incompleteness theorem, there is a theorem on undecidability over the natural numbers. This theorem states that **there is no algorithm that decides whether an arbitrary given closed logical formula is true or false in the usual interpretation over the natural numbers** [22].

Thus, even restricted to number theory, "the totality of mathematical problems" is not boring in the way the family of questions about digits of 10 / 99 is boring, since that family has an algorithm deciding YES or NO. In other words, even AI surpassing mathematicians **cannot completely solve mathematics**.

Therefore, mathematical exploration will remain **a game that continues without end** even after the birth of mathematical AI surpassing top mathematicians, and even after the Singularity. By merging with AI, humanity will be able to explore that infinite world **without being constrained by human limits**.

For example, how would number theory change if we could handle a ten-thousand-digit integer as easily as a two-digit integer? Mathematicians think not only about two- and three-dimensional objects but also about general n-dimensional and even infinite-dimensional objects; what would happen if we could handle such high-dimensional objects as if they were in our hands? There would probably be worlds there that we cannot now imagine.

The mathematical world humanity has explored so far is vast. At this point, even a genius on the scale of world history cannot know every field. But even that vast world would look like a single grain of gold dust compared with infinity.

Mathematics done by biological humans will come to an end, and a new era of mathematics will begin through humans merged with machines. At that moment, **mathematics will still have only just begun**.

## Closing

Because of the flow of the argument, there are several important topics I could not discuss sufficiently in this essay. For example, Terence Tao's view of AI [23], which differs considerably from mine, is interesting as a way to understand how a top contemporary mathematician sees the current situation.

Moreover, although I introduced superintelligence as the key to merging with AI, I was unable to discuss the debate surrounding superintelligence in detail. As a free and comprehensive Japanese-language resource on this field, I recommend [24]. The benefits of superintelligence are enormous, but so are the risks. The present moment may be the time when society after the birth of superintelligence can be influenced the most, and I hope that people with mathematical backgrounds will also join the discussion and help this field of research advance further.

I used various sources while writing this essay. In particular, information from the Singularity Server, a Discord server for discussing the Singularity, and information posted by its participants on X (Twitter) and elsewhere, was very useful for researching AI-related topics. I cited the original sources rather than those discussions directly, but I am very grateful.

Finally, the forecast developed in this essay may be difficult to accept. As a singularitarian, I think it is fairly average, but people who are not singularitarians, especially people involved in mathematics, may find it hard to accept. Therefore, there is no need to believe my forecast exactly as it stands. Instead, I hope I have conveyed that even if **machines surpass human mathematical ability, this is not something to mourn, but rather something to welcome**.
